How this CAPTCHA speed test works

This is a 60-second typing test built around the way real text CAPTCHAs behave: short, warped codes that you have to read correctly the first time. Every code is generated in your browser the moment you need it, so there is nothing to memorise and no two runs are alike.

What gets harder as you go

The difficulty tracks your number of correct answers. You start with 4-character codes and gain one character every two solves, up to 9. From your 3rd solve one symbol (@ # $ % & ? ! + =) is mixed in, and from your 6th solve there are two. Rotation, skew, letter spacing, font choice and the number of crossing noise lines all scale up too, and by roughly your 9th solve they are at maximum. Look-alike characters (0 and O, 1 and l and I) are never used, so a miss is always a misread, not a trick.

How your score is calculated

  • WPM is correctly typed characters divided by 5 (the standard length of a "word"), measured over the 60 seconds.
  • Accuracy is correct submissions divided by all submissions. A wrong answer keeps the same code on screen, so guessing fast costs you.
  • Boss round time is how long you take to tick Google's real reCAPTCHA v2 checkbox after the clock runs out. It is timed separately and does not change your WPM.

Tips for a higher score

  • Read the whole code before you type. Starting early on a 9-character code with two symbols is how most misses happen.
  • Codes are case-sensitive. Check capital letters first, because a wrong case is the most common slip.
  • Do not mash Enter on a guess. Each wrong submission lowers accuracy and wastes about two seconds.
  • Keep your eyes on the box and hands on the keyboard. Enter submits and the field clears itself, so you never need the mouse.
  • On a phone, turn off autocorrect suggestions in your keyboard if it keeps capitalising the first letter.
